\begin{abstract}
We describe a software pipeline that maps continuous-marking stochastic Petri
nets to leaky integrate-and-fire populations and a bounded actuator interface.
The implementation makes the symbolic policy, compilation products and runtime
state inspectable, but its current physical evidence is deliberately narrow.  We
evaluate the implemented PID, observer-based LQR and push--pull spiking
controllers on the same deterministic reduced-order vertical-instability model.
For a growth rate of $200\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, a 5 mm kick and a 1 kHz control
update, PID and LQR settle in 25 ms and 39 ms.  The real spiking controller does
not stabilise the case and reaches approximately 160 mm peak displacement.  Its
internal dynamics lag the 5 ms instability e-folding time.  This is a material
negative result: the current SNN is not competitive for this fast-VDE case.

We preserve the exact output of an earlier workstation-latency harness and
explain why it is historical rather than ranking evidence.  Its tracked JSON
contains five lane aggregates, but not the exact invocation episode count or
seed; it also used different plant/action paths and accepted noise and delay
arguments without injecting them.  A subsequent schema-v3 one-episode run
demonstrates common scenario, full-coil action and policy-only timing wiring,
but is explicitly promotion-ineligible: every completed lane crosses the
disruption threshold at time zero and NMPC-JAX is unavailable.  We therefore
pre-register, but do not execute, a fresh 200-episode real-kernel cohort with
fail-closed admission gates.  No FPGA, neuromorphic-device or radiation
experiment has been performed.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Petri net; spiking neural network; tokamak
vertical stability; neuromorphic control; stochastic computing; negative result

\section{Motivation and boundary}

Elongated tokamak plasmas can be unstable to the axisymmetric vertical mode,
requiring active feedback on timescales set by the resistive structure and the
closed-loop plant \citep{Lazarus1990,Humphreys2015}.  PID, state-space and
model-predictive controllers are established engineering approaches
\citep{Kolemen2014}.  Neuromorphic implementations are interesting only if
their timing, robustness and verification properties are measured under a
physically meaningful contract.

This paper is bound to commit \code{8df066b60bf7}; the full revision is in the
paper metadata.  The plant is a declared reduced-order ordinary differential
equation.  The work is not a discharge
experiment, hardware-in-the-loop validation, radiation test or production PCS.
No comparison with an independently tuned facility controller is attempted.

\section{Stochastic Petri-net representation}

A continuous-marking Petri net is represented as
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{N}=(P,T,W_{\mathrm{in}},W_{\mathrm{out}},\mathbf{m}_0),
\end{equation}
where $P$ and $T$ are finite sets of places and transitions,
$W_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $W_{\mathrm{out}}$ are non-negative arc-weight matrices,
and $\mathbf{m}_0\in[0,1]^{|P|}$ is the initial marking.  A transition is enabled
when its weighted inputs satisfy a declared threshold.  Firing consumes and
produces marking according to the two arc matrices, followed by the runtime's
explicit bounded-domain policy.

The construction is inspired by classical Petri-net semantics
\citep{Petri1962,Murata1989}, while the continuous $[0,1]$ marking is chosen to
align with stochastic-number representations \citep{Gaines1969,Alaghi2013}.
Figure~\ref{fig:petri} shows the eight-place policy used as a software fixture.
It is a control-policy graph, not a complete plasma model.

\section{Compilation and runtime}

The pipeline has three observable stages:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a builder validates places, transitions, arcs, thresholds and initial
        marking;
  \item the compiler maps places and transitions to population and connection
        structures, including stochastic bitstream representations;
  \item the runtime injects observations, advances symbolic/spiking state and
        decodes an action through the actuator boundary.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{figures/fig_compilation_pipeline.pdf}
  \caption{Software compilation path from policy graph to runtime artefact.
  Hardware targets are prospective; this draft reports software execution.}
  \label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure}

The neuron model follows a leaky integrate-and-fire form
\citep{Maass1997},
\begin{equation}
  \tau_m\frac{dV}{dt}=-(V-V_{\mathrm{rest}})+RI(t),
\end{equation}
with threshold, reset and refractory behavior.  Figure~\ref{fig:lif} is a
pedagogical deterministic trace; it is not a hardware waveform.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth]{figures/fig_lif_neuron.pdf}
  \caption{Pedagogical LIF response generated by the paper-local script.}
  \label{fig:lif}
\end{figure}

The public runtime validates finite inputs and clips decoded actions at the
actuator boundary.  Software tests exercise builder validation, compilation,
runtime behavior and safety interlocks.  They do not prove equivalence to an
unbuilt FPGA or neuromorphic circuit.

\section{Reduced-order vertical benchmark}

\subsection{Plant and protocol}

The benchmark uses the declared linearised vertical plant
\begin{equation}
  \ddot z = \gamma^2 z - \frac{k_{\mathrm{coil}}}{m_p}u,
  \label{eq:plant}
\end{equation}
with $\gamma=200\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$,
$k_{\mathrm{coil}}=0.5$, $m_p=1$, a 1 ms integration/control step and a
$\pm10$ kA command limit.  A 5 mm displacement is injected at 10 ms and the
simulation runs for 60 ms.  The 5 ms open-loop e-folding time makes this a
deliberately fast case.

The PID gains are chosen from a stated second-order target.  The LQR lane uses
the repository's observer-based state-space controller.  The SNN lane uses the
actual 64-neuron push--pull \code{SpikingControllerPool}; it is not a relabelled
PID trace.  All three trajectories are produced by
\code{figures/fig\_vertical\_stability.py}.

\section{Evaluation-contract evolution}

\subsection{Historical methodology and preserved output}

The legacy result was first tracked at commit \code{1163ce454b75} and remains
recoverable as Git blob \code{5616f192a7b4}; the exact source JSON has SHA-256
\code{394be6c32a56\ldots}.  Table~\ref{tab:historical-latency} reproduces all
five serialized p50/p95 values.  This preservation does not rehabilitate the
old comparison.

At implementation revision \code{476908debdd8}, Python PID and H-infinity used
the Python equilibrium flight simulator, Rust-PID used a separate Rust
reduced-order simulator, NMPC-JAX used an untrained random dynamics model and
returned only its first scalar action, and Nengo-SNN received the scalar error
as radial input with zero vertical input.  Latency covered a complete
lane-specific \code{run\_shot} divided by reported steps, not one policy call.
The harness accepted and printed its noise and delay arguments but did not pass
them into episode runners.  It serialized neither a seed, a common disturbance
trace, exact invocation episode count, failure ledger nor environment custody.
The code default was 1000 episodes, but the exact invocation count that produced
the tracked aggregate is unknown and is reported as unknown.

\subsection{Why the contract changed}

The historical data remain useful for auditability and for showing what the
software emitted at that time.  They cannot support a speedup, accuracy,
disruption, energy-efficiency or deployment claim because the lanes were not
accuracy-equivalent.  The replacement contract consequently requires one plant,
one R/Z observation, one full ordered coil-current-offset action in MA, the same
actuator delay/lag/slew/saturation, one pre-materialized PCG64 disturbance trace
per episode, exactly one timed policy call per control step, explicit failures,
and digest-bound recovery.

\subsection{Schema-v3 transition run}

The exact schema-v3 report at \code{8df066b60bf7} is a one-episode surrogate
wiring assessment, not a reduced-cost substitute for real-plant evidence.  Six
lanes completed against the same disturbance digest; NMPC-JAX remained visibly
unavailable because it lacks a calibrated dynamics artefact and held-out gate.
Every completed lane had the same mean radial error (4.0387 m), mean vertical
error (3.7877 m), disruption rate (1.0) and disruption time (0.0 s).  The last
quantity proves that the present initial state is already outside the declared
0.5 m criterion before actuation can discriminate between policies.

The policy-only timings in Table~\ref{tab:wiring-latency} verify instrumentation
and lane wiring only.  They cannot select a controller because the run has one

\subsection{Pre-registered fresh cohort}

The paper-local \code{fresh\_cohort\_protocol.json} freezes a 200-episode,
seed-42, 30 s, real-FusionKernel comparison across PID, H-infinity, LQR, MPC,
NMPC-JAX, LIF-NEF-SNN and Rust-PID.  The primary endpoints are disruption rate
and radial/vertical tracking error; tracking reward, actuator effort and
policy-only p50/p95/p99 latency are secondary.  Comparisons are paired by the
per-episode disturbance-trace digest, failures remain failures, and no lane can
be promoted from latency alone.

The cohort is prepared but intentionally blocked.  Before execution, the
real-kernel initial equilibrium must pass the predeclared time-zero criterion,
NMPC-JAX must load a provenance-bound calibrated model with a disjoint held-out
gate, and all seven lanes must be available.  The primary run then requires a
dedicated unloaded host and the frozen recovery identity; an independent second
host must reproduce scientific metrics while host-dependent timing is compared
separately.  No long-running fresh cohort is reported in this manuscript.

\section{Stochastic encoding and radiation boundary}

In a unipolar stochastic code, a value $x\in[0,1]$ is represented by the
fraction of ones in a bitstream of length $L$.  Independent streams allow an
AND gate to estimate multiplication.  A single flipped bit changes the decoded
value by at most $1/L$, an encoding-level sensitivity rather than a radiation
qualification result.

No FPGA timing closure, power measurement, single-event-upset campaign or
neuromorphic-device run is included.  Earlier quantitative hardware latency and
power estimates are therefore absent from the results.

\section{Limitations and next experiments}

The current evidence is bounded by the following facts:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the fast-VDE SNN result is negative;
  \item the plant is a deterministic reduced-order model and lacks a full
        plasma, vessel, power-supply, sensor and communication stack;
  \item the historical latency output lacks a serialized exact invocation
        count, seed and common lane contract;
  \item the schema-v3 transition run is a single wiring-only surrogate episode
        whose completed lanes are disrupted at time zero;
  \item NMPC-JAX has no calibrated dynamics artefact or held-out validation
        gate and is therefore unavailable;
  \item the pre-registered fresh cohort has not run and lacks independent
        second-host reproduction;
  \item symbolic/runtime tests are not a hardware refinement proof;
  \item radiation and dedicated-hardware properties are unmeasured;
  \item no experimental control, hardware-in-the-loop or production PCS claim
        is made.
\end{itemize}

The next credible sequence is to freeze an accuracy-equivalent reduced-order
controller cohort, reproduce it independently, add sensor/actuator and delay
uncertainty, and only then synthesize and measure a hardware candidate.  A
device experiment must report timing, power, numerical/control quality and fault
injection together.

\section{Conclusion}

The Petri-net-to-spiking path is implemented and inspectable, but the current
SNN does not stabilise the declared fast vertical-instability case.  This result
keeps the software architecture distinct from demonstrated control performance.
The paper-local evidence preserves the legacy record without treating it as a
current ranking, exposes the failed schema-v3 admission state, and pre-registers
the next accuracy-equivalent study without claiming that it has run.
