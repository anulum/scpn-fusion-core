\begin{abstract}
SCPN-Fusion-Core contains a linear resistive reduced-MHD eigenvalue solver for
a Harris current sheet and a periodic cylinder, discretised with C1 cubic
Hermite elements.  Its unstable roots are found by a dense QZ solve or by a
verified shift-and-invert search.  We test it against nine acceptance criteria
fixed before the run, built on the asymptotic growth rates of resistive
tearing (scaling as $\eta^{3/5}$) and of the large-$\Delta'$ limit (scaling as
$\eta^{1/3}$).  Seven criteria pass.  For the Harris sheet the growth rate
follows $\eta^{0.598}$ and matches the asymptotic constant-$\psi$ rate to
0.2\% at $\eta = 10^{-8}$.  The cylindrical resistive kink follows
$\eta^{0.306}$ and matches its asymptotic rate to 3.1\%.  The cylindrical 2/1
tearing mode matches the asymptotic rate to 4.9\% at $\eta = 10^{-8}$, using a
stability index $\Delta' = 9.69$ from an independent shooting calculation.
Two criteria are missed, and we report them as misses.  The cylindrical
tearing slope over $\eta = 10^{-6}$ to $10^{-8}$ is 0.554, below its band of
0.56--0.64.  A Harris sheet with $\Delta' < 0$ shows a root of $7.9 \times
10^{-9}$, above the fixed limit of $10^{-9}$ though four orders of magnitude
below the tearing rate at the same resistivity.  A follow-up scan to $\eta =
10^{-10}$ explains the first miss: the local slope rises to 0.595 and the
growth rate approaches the asymptotic rate, as expected when $\Delta'$ times
the layer width becomes small.  The second is not removed: the stray root
does not converge as the grid is refined, and a diagnostic shows that its sign
is not determined in double precision, because the near-zero eigenvalues of
the discretised problem have condition numbers up to $1.9 \times 10^{12}$.
The solver has no toroidal terms and no nonlinear physics.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} tearing mode; resistive kink; reduced MHD; Hermite
finite elements; eigenvalue problem; verification

\section{Introduction}

Linear resistive stability codes are verified against the asymptotic theory
of the tearing instability.  In the constant-$\psi$ limit first treated by
Furth, Killeen and Rosenbluth \citep{FKR1963}, the growth rate is $\lambda =
C^{-4/5} \eta^{3/5} \Delta'^{4/5}$ with $C = \pi \Gamma(3/4) / (2 \Gamma(5/4))
\approx 1.45$; when $\Delta'$ is large it becomes $\lambda^3 = \eta$, a growth
rate that scales as $\eta^{1/3}$ \citep{BoldyrevLoureiro2018}.  We describe a
linear reduced-MHD solver and test it against these limits with criteria fixed
in advance, including the two that it misses.

\section{The solver}

The solver advances the linear resistive reduced-MHD system
\begin{equation}
\lambda \psi = i F \phi + \eta L \psi, \qquad
\lambda L \phi = i F L \psi - i \kappa_b J_0' \psi,
\end{equation}
for the perturbed flux $\psi$ and stream function $\phi$, with $F$ the
parallel wave number of the equilibrium field, $L$ the perpendicular
Laplacian, and $\kappa_b J_0'$ the drive by the equilibrium current gradient, in a Harris slab ($B_y = \tanh
x$) or a periodic cylinder.  The radial direction uses C1 cubic Hermite
elements, clustered at the resonant surface.  For at most 2000 unknowns a
dense QZ solve finds every root.  Larger problems use shift-and-invert Arnoldi
iteration over a ladder of real shifts; every root is refined by inverse
iteration and verified by its eigenvector residual.  That search is marked
incomplete, and finding no root means none in the searched domain, not
stability.  The stability index $\Delta'$ of the cylinder comes from a separate
shooting calculation of the outer ideal solution.

\section{Acceptance}

The criteria and their bands were fixed in the acceptance module before the
run.  Growth rates are in Alfv\'en units at resistivities $\eta = 10^{-6}$,
$10^{-7}$ and $10^{-8}$ (Lundquist numbers $10^6$ to $10^8$); a slope is the
least-squares exponent of the growth rate in $\eta$ over the three values.  The
Harris sheet uses $ka = 0.5$ (unstable, $\Delta' > 0$) and $ka = 1.2$
($\Delta' < 0$) on a domain of half-width 20.  The cylinder carries the current
$J_0 = j_0 (1 - r^2)^\nu$ with $B_z = 1$ and major radius 10: $q(0) = 1.2$ and
$\nu = 1.5$ for the 2/1 tearing mode, and $q(0) = 0.9$ and $\nu = 1$ for the
1/1 resistive kink, whose reference rate is $\eta^{1/3} |F'|^{2/3}$ at the
resonant surface.

\section{The two misses}

The cylindrical 2/1 slope of 0.554 lies below its band.  The growth rates at
the three resistivities are $3.05 \times 10^{-4}$, $8.86 \times 10^{-5}$ and
$2.37 \times 10^{-5}$.  The constant-$\psi$ rate is the limit of small
$\Delta'$ times the resistive-layer width, and with $\Delta' = 9.69$ that
product is not yet small at $\eta = 10^{-6}$.  This is a plausible reason for
the shallower slope, and a follow-up study tests it by extending the scan to
$\eta = 10^{-10}$.  The local slope between neighbouring resistivities rises
from 0.537 to 0.572, 0.588 and 0.595.  The ratio of the growth rate to the
asymptotic rate rises from 0.772 through 0.951 to 0.991.  Both approach their
constant-$\psi$ values as $\eta$ falls, as the explanation predicts.  At $\eta
= 10^{-10}$ the growth rate is $1.56 \times 10^{-6}$, and doubling the
elements per side changes it by a relative $1.7 \times 10^{-4}$.  The
acceptance criterion itself is unchanged and is still missed over its three
resistivities.

The Harris sheet with $ka = 1.2$, for which $\Delta' < 0$, should have no
growing tearing root.  The solver finds one at $7.9 \times 10^{-9}$, above the
fixed limit of $10^{-9}$.  That root is more than four orders of magnitude
below the $ka = 0.5$ tearing rate at the same resistivity ($2.48 \times
10^{-4}$).  The follow-up study repeats the search at $\eta = 10^{-6}$,
$10^{-7}$ and $10^{-8}$ with 200 and 400 elements per side, each time as a
dense QZ solve over all finite eigenvalues.  The acceptance's own search turns
iterative above 2000 unknowns, which a 400-element problem exceeds, so the
dense solve is forced to compare like with like.  With 200 elements the roots
are $7.92 \times 10^{-9}$, $1.05 \times 10^{-8}$ and $1.36 \times 10^{-8}$.
With 400 elements they are $1.04 \times 10^{-9}$, 0 and $1.86 \times
10^{-7}$; the last is 1.2\% of the $ka = 0.5$ tearing rate of $1.58 \times
10^{-5}$.  The root does not converge as the grid is refined, and at 200
elements it grows rather than shrinks as $\eta$ falls.  Both point to spurious
eigenvalues of the discretised problem rather than a physical mode.

A diagnostic of the 200-element pencil at $\eta = 10^{-6}$ shows why.  The
matrix $A$ is numerically singular: its singular values run from $1.5 \times
10^{-17}$ to $1.3 \times 10^{2}$, and 158 finite eigenvalues have modulus
below $10^{-4}$.  For the twelve smallest, the condition numbers computed from
their left and right eigenvectors range from $1.6 \times 10^{10}$ to $1.9
\times 10^{12}$.  Their first-order error bounds, $4.6 \times 10^{-4}$ to
$5.6 \times 10^{-2}$, therefore lie far above the stray root, while their
residuals are at most $1.5 \times 10^{-14}$; a residual check cannot detect
the problem.  On the host of the diagnostic the largest real part is $6.05
\times 10^{-10}$ rather than the acceptance's $7.9 \times 10^{-9}$, itself a
sign of the same sensitivity.  Relative perturbations of $10^{-15}$ to $A$
move it to $-8.9 \times 10^{-12}$, $3.8 \times 10^{-11}$ and $6.2 \times
10^{-10}$.  The sign of the stray root is therefore not determined in double
precision, and the limit of $10^{-9}$ lies below the accuracy this pencil
attains.  The limit was not changed after the run, and the criterion stays a
miss.  A criterion that is well posed in finite precision is left for future
work.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item Slab and periodic-cylinder geometry only.  The solver has no toroidal
$1/R$ terms and no Grad--Shafranov equilibrium, and it covers linear physics
only.
\item The acceptance covers three resistivities.  A follow-up scan explains
the slope miss.  The stray Harris root lies below the accuracy the pencil
attains in double precision, so this solver cannot decide that criterion as it
was fixed.
\item The run times recorded with the report describe one host and are not a
performance result.
\end{itemize}
